## Introduction

For my master's thesis, I decided to dive into the world of topological insulators, this led me to the applications of Non-commutative Geometry into homogeneous materials a.k.a. disordered crystals. We followed [Prodan et.al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.08744) on how K-theory and Cyclic cohomology provides topological invariants for Hamiltonians describing disordered crystals. The results from Prodan et.al. also provide a proof for the quantization of such invariants under weak disorder. For more information refer to my [thesis](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1VXjomd_6Oim7oigK2n3sZYwsrhWClumS).

## Math

For our intends and purposes the Non-Commutative Brillouin torus is a non-commutative smooth manifold, in which will reside the Hamiltonians of the disordered crystals. In the context of Non-Commutative Geometry, we translate topological/geometrical concepts into algebraic/analytic ones, thus, while the Brillouin torus is the torus $\mathbb{T}^n$ with its smooth and topological features, then Non-Commutative Brillouin torus corresponds to a pair of topological algebras, one with the topological information (a C* algebra), and the other with the smooth structure (a smooth sub-algebra). The C* algebra takes the form of a twisted crossed product, which we denote by $A \rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta}\mathbb{Z}^d$, and the smooth sub algebra, which we denote by $\mathcal{A}_{\alpha, \zeta}$, takes the form of a dense sub *algebra of $A \rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta}\mathbb{Z}^d$ that is invariant under the holomorphic functional calculus of $A \rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta}\mathbb{Z}^d$ and has a Frechet topology that is stronger than the topology of $A \rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta}\mathbb{Z}^d$.

The twisted crossed product is a C* algebra that is constructed from a twisted dynamical system, whose ingredients are:

- A nuclear C* algebra $A$, in our case we will take $A = C(\Omega)$ with $\Omega$ a compact, contractible and second countable Hausdorff space, also, we ask for $\Omega$ to have a Haar measure with full support
- An abelian group $G$, in our case we work with $G = \mathbb{Z}^n$
- An action $\alpha$ of $G$ over $A$
- A bounded cocycle $\zeta$ over $G$, in our case, the bounded cocycles look like $\zeta(x,y) = \exp(i \bra{x} \Theta \ket{y})$ with $\Theta$ a lower triangular matrix with entries in $[-\pi, \pi)$ and zeros in the diagonal

We have the following comparative between then Brillouin torus and the Non-Commutative Brillouin torus,

| **Brillouin Torus** | **Non-Commutative Brillouin torus** |

| Every $f \in C(\mathbb{T}^d)$ is uniquely determined by a set $\{ \mathcal{F}(f)(s) \}_{s \in \mathbb{Z}^d}, \; \mathcal{F}(f)(s) \in \mathbb{C},$ where $\mathcal{F}(f)(s) = \int_{\mathbb{T}^d} \gamma_{-s}(\lambda) f(\lambda) d \mu(\lambda)$. In this expression, $\gamma_{-s}(\lambda)$ is the character over $\mathbb{T}^d$ given by $\lambda^{-s}$ | Every $p \in C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha, \zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d$ is uniquely determined by a set $\{ \Phi_s (p) \}_{s \in \mathbb{Z}^d}, \; \Phi_s(p) \in C(\Omega),$ where $\Phi_s(p) u^s = \int_{\mathbb{T}^d} \gamma_{-s}(\lambda) \tau(\lambda)(p) d \mu(\lambda).$ In this expression, $\tau$ is a strongly continuous action of $\mathbb{Z}^d$ over $C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha, \zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d$ and $\gamma_{-s}(\lambda)$ is the character over $\mathbb{T}^d$ given by $\lambda^{-s}$. |
| $C(\mathbb{T}^d) \simeq \underbrace{C(\mathbb{T}) \otimes \cdots \otimes C(\mathbb{T})}_{d \text{ times }},$ and for any $1 \leq j \leq d-1$, $C(\mathbb{T}^j)$ is a sub C* algebra of $C(\mathbb{T}^d)$. | $C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d$ can be written as an iterated crossed product with $\mathbb{Z}$, that is, there are the d canonical C* algebras $\{ A_i \}_{0 \leq i \leq d-1}$ and group actions $\alpha_i : \mathbb{Z} \to \text{Aut}(A_{i-1})$ such that $A_{i-1} \simeq A_i \rtimes_{\alpha_i} \mathbb{Z}$ and $C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha, \zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d \simeq A_{d-1} \rtimes_{\alpha_d} \mathbb{Z}$ with $C(\Omega) = A_0$. | $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^d)$ is Fréchet m-convex algebra with $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^d) \subset C(\mathbb{T}^d),$ such that, $f \in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^d)$ iff for every $n \in \mathbb{N}^d$ there is $K_n < \infty$ with $\|s\|^n \| \mathcal{F}(f)(s) \| < K_n.$ Additionally, $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^d)$ is invariant under the holomorphic functional calculus of $C(\mathbb{T}^d)$, and the set of self-adjoint elements of $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^d)$ is invariant under the smooth functional calculus on $\mathbb{R}$ defined over $C(\mathbb{T}^d)$. | There is Fréchet m-convex algebra $\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta}$ with $\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha, \zeta} \subset C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha, \zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d,$ such that, $p \in \mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha, \zeta}$ iff for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ there is $K_n < \infty$ with $\|s\|^n \\| \Phi_s(p) \\| < K_n.$ Additionally, $\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta}$ is invariant under the holomorphic functional calculus of $C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d$, and the set of self adjoint elements of $\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha, \zeta}$ is invariant under the smooth functional calculus on $\mathbb{R}$ definer over $C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha, \zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d$. |

Notice that the Non-Commutative Brillouin torus falls back into the Brillouin torus when $\alpha$ is trivial and $\zeta$ is trivial i.e. when we take away the non-commutativity on the generators of the Non-Commutative Brillouin torus.

Let $A$ be a C* algebra and $\mathcal{A}$ a smooth sub algebra of $A$, then, the groups $K_0(A), \; K_0(\mathcal{A})$ are constructed from homotopy classes of projections in matrix algebras of $A$ and $\mathcal{A}$ respectively. The groups $K_1(A), \; K_1(\mathcal{A})$ are constructed from homotopy classes of unitaries in matrix algebras of $A$ and $\mathcal{A}$ respectively. Turns out that the homotopy classes of projections/unitaires of smooth sub algebras are in one to one correspondence with the homotopy classes of their C* algebras, thus, there are isomorphisms

$$
K_0(A) \simeq K_0(\mathcal{A}), \; K_1(A) \simeq K_1(\mathcal{A}).
$$

Using the Toeplitz-extension associated to a crossed product with $\mathbb{Z}$ is possible to compute the the K groups $C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d$, which happen to take the following form

$$
K_j(C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha, \zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d) = \mathbb{Z}^{2^{d-1}}, \; j = 0,1 \; .
$$

Using the derivations and continuous trace over $\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta}$ we can define the following continuous cyclic cocycles ,

$$
\xi_I (p_0, \cdots, p_n) = \Lambda_n \sum_{\rho \in S_n} (-1)^{\rho} \left(\int \circ \Phi_0 \right) (p_0 (\partial_{\rho_1} p_1) \cdots (\partial_{\rho_n} p_n) )
$$

where $I = \{i_1, \cdots, i_n \} \subset \{ 1, \cdots, d \}$ is an ordered subset with $n$ elements, $S_n$ is the symmetric group of degree $n$ and $\rho \in S_n$ is a bijection between $\{ 1, \cdots, n \}$ and $I$ with signature $(-1)^{\rho}$.

Denote by $HC_{\text{con}}^*(\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta})$ the continuous cyclic cohomology of $\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta}$, so, given that $HC_{\text{con}}^*(\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta})$ is composed of equivalence classes of continuous cyclic cocycles, we can piggy back on the pairing between $K_j(\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta}), \; j=0,1$ and $HC_{\text{con}}^*(\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta})$ to provide topological invariants for the Non-Commuative Brullouin torus. In particular, the pairing takes the following form,

- If $p$ is a projection then its equivalence class in $K_0(C(\Omega)\rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta}\mathbb{Z}^d)$ can be paired with even cyclic cocycles in the following way,

$$
\langle [p], [\xi_I] \rangle = \Lambda_{|I|} \sum_{\rho \in S_{|I|}} (-1)^{\rho} \int_{\Omega} \text{tr} \left( \Phi_0 ( p (\partial_{\rho_1} p) \cdots (\partial_{\rho_{|I|}} p) )(\omega) \right) d \mu(\omega), \; p \in P_n(\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta}) ,
$$

- If u is an unitary then its equivalence class in $K_1(C(\Omega)\rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta}\mathbb{Z}^d)$ can be paired with the odd cyclic cocycles in the following way

$$
\langle [u], [\xi_I] \rangle = \Lambda_{|I|} \sum_{\rho \in S_{|I|}} (-1)^{\rho} \int_{\omega \in \Omega} \text{tr} \left( \Phi_0 \left(\prod_{l = 1}^{{|I|}} u^* (\partial_{\rho_l} u) \right)(\omega) \right) d \mu(\omega), \; u \in U_n(\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta}).
$$

When $I$ has an even number of elements the pairing $\langle [p], [\xi_I] \rangle$ becomes a generalization of the even Chern numbers, similarly, if $I$ has a an odd number of elements the pairing $\langle [u], [\xi_I] \rangle$ becomes a generalization of the odd Chern numbers, thus, we will use the notation

- Non-commutative Even Chern numbers: $\text{Ch}_I : K_0(C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d) \to \mathbb{C}, \; |I| = 2n$ with $\text{Ch}_{I}([p]) := \langle [p], [\xi_I] \rangle, \; p \in P_n(\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta})$.
- Non-commutative Even Chern numbers $\text{Ch}_I : K_1(C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d) \to \mathbb{C}, \; |I| = 2n + 1$ with $\text{Ch}_{I}([u]) := \langle [u], [\xi_I] \rangle, \; u \in U_n(\mathcal{C}(\Omega)_{\alpha,\zeta})$.

The range of the Non-commutative Chern numbers takes the following form

$$
\text{Ran}(\text{Ch}_I) = \mathbb{Z} + \sum_{I \subset J} (2 \pi)^{- \frac{1}{2} |J \setminus I |} \text{Pf}(\tilde{\Theta}_{J \setminus I}) \mathbb{Z}
$$

with

- $J \setminus I$ is the set difference between the multi-indexes $J$ and $I$
- $\tilde{\Theta}$ is real anti-symmetric matrix whose lower triangular part is $\Theta$, and $\zeta(x,y) = \exp(i \bra{x} \Theta \ket{y})$
- $\tilde{\Theta}_{J \setminus I}$ is the square matrix obtained from $\tilde{\Theta}$ by restricting to only the rows and columns whose indices correspond to the elements of $J \setminus I$
- $|I|$ is the cardinality of $I$
- the sum $\sum_{I \subset J}$ goes over $J$'s with $|J|$ even.

Consequently, if

- $d=2$ then $\text{Ran}(\text{Ch}_{\{1,2\}}) = \mathbb{Z}$
- $d=3$ and $|I|=2$ then $\text{Ran}(\text{Ch}_I) = \mathbb{Z}$

these two results lie behind the quantization of the traversal conductivity in the Integer Quantum Hall Effect (IQHE).

## Physics

We will work with effective models over lattices, these are called tight-binding models. The Fourier analysis is a powerful tool to analyze tight-binding models over periodic lattices a.k.a. periodic crystals, because it allows to translate problems from $L^2(\mathbb{Z}^d)\otimes \mathbb{C}^n$ into $L^2(\mathbb{T}^d)\otimes \mathbb{C}^n$ , that is, we go from a Hamiltonian over a lattice to an equivalent operator over the $n$ dimensional Brillouin zone $L^2(\mathbb{T}^d) \otimes \mathbb{C}^n \simeq (L^2(\mathbb{T}^d))^n$. We are interested in translation invariant Hamiltonians, those take the form

$$
H = \sum_{y \in \mathbb{Z}^d} W_y S^y, \; \text{a finte sum,}
$$

with $S^y \in B(L^2(\mathbb{Z}^d)), \; S^y \ket{x} = \ket{x+y}$ . A Hamiltonian over $L^2(\mathbb{Z}^d) \otimes \mathbb{C}$ describes a system without boundaries, which is also know as a bulk model. These types of systems describe perfect crystals, thus, are not well suited for dealing with elements that contain random imperfections. Besides the random imperfections in the crystals we would like to take into account the effect of a magnetic field, henceforth, we need to modify the form of the Hamiltonians.

We will work with crystals containing random imperfections, and we care about the macroscopic features that stay invariant under those random imperfections, therefore, we will pass from working with one Hamiltonian to work with a covariant family of Hamiltonians. A covariant family of Hamiltonians looks like

$$
H = \{ H_{\omega} \}_{\omega \in \Omega}, \; \text{such that, } V^y H_{\omega} (V^y)^* = H_{\alpha(y)(\omega)}, \; \forall y \in \mathbb{Z}^d
$$

with $\alpha: \mathbb{Z}^d \to \text{Aut}(\Omega)$ a group action an a compact, contractible and second countable Hausdorff space. In the previous setting $V^y$ are direct magnetic translation, and take the form

$$
V^y := e^{i \bra{X} B_+ \ket{y}} S^y, \text{ that is, } \; V^y \ket{x} = e^{i \bra{x+y} B_+ \ket{y}} \ket{x + y},
$$

with $B_+$ a the lower triangular part of a real anti-symmetric matrix having entries on $[-\pi, \pi)$, in this setting $B_+$ contains the effect of a constant magnetic field over the disordered crystal. The prototypical form of $H$ is $\{ \sum_{y \in \mathcal{R}} W_y(\omega) \otimes U^y\}_{\omega \in \Omega}$ with $W_y : \Omega \to M_n(\mathbb{C})$ continuous functions, and $U^y$ the dual magnetic translations i.e.

$$
U^y := S^y e^{i \bra{y} B_+ \ket{X}}, \text{ that is, } U^y \ket{x} = e^{i \bra{y} B_+ \ket{x}} \ket{x + y}.
$$

The action $\alpha$ induces and action $\tilde{\alpha}: \mathbb{Z}^d \to \text{Aut}(C(\Omega))$ with $\tilde{\alpha}(y)(f)(\omega):= f(\alpha(-y)(\omega)),\; y \in \mathbb{Z}^d$, such that, if $H = \{ H_{\omega}\}_{\omega \in \Omega}$ is a covariant family of Hamiltonians then $H \in M_n(C(\Omega) \rtimes_{\tilde{\alpha},\zeta}\mathbb{Z}^d)$.

In the literature the most usually from that $\Omega$ takes is

$$
\Omega = \prod_{s \in \mathbb{Z}^d} \Omega_0, \; \alpha(y): \Omega \to \Omega, \; \alpha(y)\left((l \mapsto \omega_l)_{l \in \mathbb{Z}^d}\right) = (l \mapsto \omega_{l - y})_{l \in \mathbb{Z}^d},
$$

with $\Omega_0$ a topological space that parameterize the disorder associated to the interactions with a given set of nearest neighbors on the tight-binding model. This $\Omega$ comes motivated from the disorder introduced in a pure crystal due to thermal fluctuations. For example, if we want to work the closest neighbors inside the cube of side $2L$, we can take $\Omega_0 = \prod_{y \in V_L} \Omega_0^y, \; V_L = \{ (y_1, \cdots, y_d) \in \mathbb{Z}^d | |y_i| \leq L \}$ with $\Omega_0^y$ a compact, metrizable and convex space for all $y \in V_L$.

Let $H = \{ H_{\omega} \}_{\omega \in \Omega} \in C(\Omega)\rtimes_{\alpha,\zeta} \mathbb{Z}^d \otimes M_n(\mathbb{C})$ be a covariant family of Hamiltonians with a gap $g$ in its spectrum, if the Fermi levels lies in that gap ($\mu \in g$) then, in the limit of zero temperature and infinite relaxation time, we have that

$$
\lim _{\beta \uparrow \infty} f_{\beta, \mu}(H)=P_F, \text{ with } f_{\beta, \mu}(H)=\frac{1}{1+\exp (\beta(H-\mu))}
$$

with $P_F = \chi(H \leq \mu)$ and $\chi(x) = 1 \text{ if } x \leq \mu \text{ else } \chi(x) = 0$. Also, the non-diagonal elements the conductivity tensor take the following form,

$$
\sigma_{i,j} = \langle [P_F], [\xi_{\{i,j\}}] \rangle = \text{Ch}_{\{i,j \}}(P_F)
$$

therefore, the non-diagonal elements of the conductivity tensor are topological invariants associated to $H$ as long as the spectral gap is not closed. When a physical quantity is invariant as long as the spectral gap around the Fermi levels is not closed it is said to be invariant under weak disorder. Moreover, if $d=2$ the range of the Non-commutative even Chern numbers tell us that the range of non-diagonal elements of the conductivity tensor are quantized and take values in $\mathbb{Z}$, which is known as the Integer Quantum Hall Effect in $2d$. Something similar happens if $d=3$, which leads to the Integer Quantum Hall effect in $3d$.

When $d=1,2$ and the family of Hamiltonians $H$ have chiral symmetry, the Non-Commutative odd Chern numbers provide a way to compute the chiral polarization at the boundary of semi-infinite systems, and such polarization turns out to be quantized.

These results on quantization of physical observables and their invariance under weak disorder are the tip of the iceberg in terms of the applications of Non-Commutative Geometry to solid sate physics, for more information you can consult
